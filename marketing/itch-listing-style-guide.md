# itch.io Listing Style Guide for Cloudy Ninja

Research-only deliverable for ticket **T-048**. Compiled from 12 itch.io listings sampled across three reference buckets: Celeste-style precision platformers, Metroidvanias / pixel-art exploration, and eco / nature-themed games. Pages were fetched once each on 2026-05-12; ratings and prices reflect that snapshot and will drift.

The goal: figure out what a well-converting itch.io page looks like, then specify what Cloudy Ninja's listing should look like at alpha.

## (b) Recommended headline-copy patterns

Five patterns repeatedly appear in the reference set. Each example is verbatim from the listing.

### Pattern 1 — Verb + Protagonist + World ("help X do Y in Z")
The most common, used by both the highest-rated paid game (Celeste) and a top-rated free one (Sheepy).
- _"Help Madeline survive her inner demons on her journey to the top of Celeste Mountain..."_ (Celeste)
- _"Help Sheepy, an abandoned plushy brought to life, as he explores a forgotten and broken world."_ (Sheepy)
- _"In Alwa's Awakening you play as Zoe, a heroine sent from another world to bring peace to the land of Alwa."_ (Alwa's Awakening)

Why it works: gives the reader a name to root for and a verb to imagine doing, in the same sentence.

### Pattern 2 — Imperative verb stack ("Verb. Verb. Verb.")
Player actions promised, no protagonist setup.
- _"Hike, climb, and soar through the peaceful mountainside landscapes of Hawk Peak Provincial Park."_ (A Short Hike)
- _"Explore an ancient world, acquire new abilities and discover secrets!"_ (Lone Fungus, second sentence)
- _"GO FAST, HIT THINGS"_ (Vapor Trails — caps imperative, two words)

Why it works: every verb is something the player wants to be doing.

### Pattern 3 — "X-like" anchor
Names a well-known reference game so the player can pattern-match in one second.
- _"A unique Zelda-like game, influenced by games such as Yume Nikki and Link's Awakening."_ (Anodyne)
- _"super-tight, hand-crafted platformer from the creators of multiplayer classic TowerFall."_ (Celeste — credit-anchor variant)

Use with care: itch.io reviewers like genre clarity, but lazy "Celeste-like" without follow-up reads as derivative.

### Pattern 4 — Concept inversion ("a reverse X")
A one-line hook that flips an expectation. Often paired with a longer second sentence.
- _"A reverse city builder"_ → _"A game about transforming a barren, lifeless landscape into a thriving, vibrant ecosystem."_ (Terra Nil)
- _"Lone Fungus is a true Metroidvania set in a world built by mushrooms where you play as the last mushroom alive."_ (Lone Fungus — inversion via setting)

Why it works: pattern interrupt. The reader stops scrolling.

### Pattern 5 — Mood-first poetic fragment
Tiny tagline that sets emotional register before any mechanics talk. Common on pay-what-you-want art-leaning pages.
- _"_who are you_"_ (SELF)
- _"it's fall in _froggo town_ / time for a one final round / to catch some flies!"_ (Frogfall — haiku)
- _"When reality feels like it's falling apart, one might retreat to a world in their dreams to escape it."_ (Pseudoregalia)

Why it works: signals "this is a piece of art, not a content product". Strongest when the visuals back the mood.

## (c) Screenshot composition rules

From the 12 listings, the patterns that clearly correlate with high ratings:

### What works
1. **Lead with a GIF, not a still.** Frogfall, A Short Hike, Vapor Trails, Sheepy, and Öoo all open with motion. itch.io's gallery autoplays the first asset — a 2-3 second loop sells the game-feel faster than any sentence.
2. **Hero shot must contain the character.** Sheepy, Celeste, A Short Hike, Lone Fungus all put the protagonist at readable size in their first frame. Pages that lead with empty environment (Anodyne, parts of SELF) rate lower.
3. **Biome variety in the gallery, not the hero.** Demon Slayer's 16 stills walk through distinct biomes. Terra Nil's 11 stills tell a barren-to-lush arc. Use the gallery as a montage, not a portfolio dump.
4. **Animated GIFs > YouTube embeds for itch.io specifically.** Of 12 listings only 1 had a clear video embed. GIFs autoplay silently in the gallery; YouTube requires a click and breaks the page rhythm.
5. **Match the gallery's pixel scale.** Every top-rated page presents screenshots at native or integer-scaled resolution. No anti-aliased downscales.

### What doesn't
1. **Cluttered hero shots with HUD overlay.** Several lower-rated listings (not in the top set) show the full HUD in their hero. The hero should show the world; HUD belongs on screenshot #3+.
2. **Pre-rendered key art with no in-game pixel.** itch.io reviewers are pixel-purists; key art that looks like a Steam capsule but doesn't match the game disappoints. Anodyne sidesteps this by leading with in-game environments.
3. **More than ~10 screenshots without a story.** Demon Slayer gets away with 16 because it's selling biome breadth; most games shouldn't. 5-7 is the sweet spot in the sample.
4. **Crowded layouts with text on the screenshot.** Not seen in any of the high-rated references. Text belongs in the page body.

**Hero-image dimensions:** itch.io renders cover images at 630×500 and gallery screenshots at native size. Plan a dedicated 630×500 cover (separate from in-game screenshots) and design it as a poster, not a wide-screen grab.

## (d) Trailer structure recommendation

Most top-rated itch.io pages in the sample do not embed a trailer at all — they rely on autoplaying GIFs. Cloudy Ninja should still produce one (it's the asset everyone wants for outside-itch.io promo), but it should be optimized for short attention.

**Recommended length:** 60-75 seconds. Long enough for three character switches and one boss tease; short enough to share in a tweet/short. (Reference scene-length: A Short Hike and Frogfall both make their case in ~3-second GIF loops.)

**Beat sheet:**
1. **0:00-0:05 — Hook.** Cold open on the most visually striking moment: a corrupted-to-restored ecosystem transformation. No logo, no text. Just the world.
2. **0:05-0:25 — Gameplay clarity.** Show one character doing one ability cleanly. Then the second. Then a switch mid-air to the third. The switching IS the demo.
3. **0:25-0:50 — Feature montage.** Three short clips: a new biome, the Storm Sentinel boss silhouette, a quick assist-mode toggle on-screen.
4. **0:50-1:05 — Stakes / scope.** Title card with the headline copy, one earned testimonial or solo-dev credit, achievements/save slots flashed.
5. **1:05-1:15 — CTA.** "Play the alpha on itch.io" + URL on a held frame. No fade. No music outro tail beyond half a second.

**Music style:** Mirror the in-game ambient score, not a generic energetic indie-trailer track. The score is part of the differentiator — using stock orchestral hype music undersells the eco-mood. Pick a single in-game cue with a clear melodic hook and let it carry the whole trailer. If a vocal/lyric layer ever exists, save it for the 0:25 montage beat.

**Captions:** Open captions baked in for the first 5 seconds (so the cold open reads with sound off). After that, leave the picture clean.

## (e) Three differentiators for Cloudy Ninja's listing

The pitch boils down to: **climate-restoration narrative + three switchable elemental characters + accessibility-first design**. Each needs a dedicated surface on the page.

### 1. Climate / ecosystem restoration angle
Terra Nil owns the eco-restoration framing on itch.io and rates 4.7 — proof the audience exists, but it's a strategy game. A pixel-art *platformer* with the same theme is open territory.

**How to surface it:**
- **Headline (pattern 4, concept inversion):** lead with something like _"A platformer where you heal the level instead of fighting through it."_ Then expand into the protagonist's setup in a second sentence.
- **Cover image:** split-frame composition — left half corrupted (desaturated, glitchy palette), right half restored (full saturation, ambient particles). Sells the loop in a single thumbnail.
- **First gallery GIF:** a 3-second restoration-transition clip. Same screen, before/after — this is the Terra Nil move, in a platformer frame.

### 2. Three-character switching (Ebo / Laya / Zephyr)
The water-cycle ability triad is mechanically unusual on itch.io platformers — none of the 12 references swap protagonists mid-level. This is the strongest one-screenshot hook.

**How to surface it:**
- **Dedicated GIF #2:** mid-air character switch chained into a traversal that needs all three (e.g. Laya makes the cloud, Zephyr rides the wind, Ebo lands as rain). One loop, no captions needed.
- **Body copy headline pattern 2 (verb stack):** _"Freeze, drift, pour — switch between three water-cycle heroes mid-jump to reach what one of them couldn't."_
- **Tags to add:** `Pixel Art`, `Platformer`, `Climate`, `Accessibility`, plus a custom-feeling one — `multi-character` or `character-switch` — to be findable when itch.io's tag pages get scraped by recommendation sites.

### 3. Accessibility-first design (assist mode, rebinding, scaling)
Assist mode is what unlocked Celeste's mainstream review love. None of the other 11 references explicitly market accessibility on the listing — most don't even mention controller support. Cloudy Ninja can claim this space cheaply.

**How to surface it:**
- **A dedicated "Accessibility" callout box** in the body, above the system requirements. List concrete items: assist mode, full key/button rebinding, 4K/HiDPI scaling, 12 achievements that work in assist mode, 3 save slots, mobile + desktop. Specifics out-convert generic claims like "accessible to all".
- **Apply the `assist-mode` and `accessibility-blind` (if applicable) itch.io tags.** Both are live tag pages with active filtering audiences.
- **One screenshot showing the assist-mode menu open.** Subtle, but it tells reviewers writing roundups that the feature exists and is integrated, not bolted on. Frame it like Celeste did — accessibility as design, not concession.

## Snapshot of what the listing should look like at alpha

If everything above is followed, the top of `cloudyninja.itch.io/cloudy-ninja` reads, roughly:

> **Cloudy Ninja** — _Heal a broken world, one cloud at a time._
>
> A platformer where you restore corrupted ecosystems instead of fighting through them. Switch between three water-cycle heroes — Ebo, Laya, and Zephyr — mid-jump to reach what one of them couldn't. Eight levels, a Storm Sentinel boss, 12 achievements, full assist mode.

Followed by: 630×500 split-frame cover, 5-7 gallery assets (lead GIF: restoration transition; second GIF: mid-air character switch; remaining stills: biome variety + one assist-menu screenshot), embedded 60-75s trailer, accessibility callout box, pay-what-you-want or low-tier price during alpha to maximize review velocity, and a clearly labeled "Alpha build — feedback welcome" banner at the bottom of the description.
